CASE 05 // CUSTOMER ENGINEERING

NEOM: Simulating
design decisions.

An interactive Unreal Engine simulation that helped architects and a lead designer evaluate environmental and design scenarios before construction.

The problem

The client needed a realistic, interactive simulation of a planned physical environment so the design team could evaluate conditions before construction. The simulation had to be useful for actual design decisions, not just a visual demo.

My contribution

I was responsible for two technical systems within the simulation: a dynamic lighting model for non-player characters and a configurable environmental evaluation framework.

01 / Dynamic lighting

I implemented sun-to-character raycasting and material-aware light attenuation. Intersected objects affected light transmission according to material tags, while solar intensity and sun position varied with the simulated date and time using location-specific meteorological data.

02 / Scenario evaluation

I built a configurable matrix for environmental variables such as speaker placement, sound effects, wall colors, daylight levels, glass transparency, and shade positions. This let the design team compare scenarios systematically instead of relying on ad hoc visual checks.

03 / Fidelity under constraints

Performance optimization could not compromise design intent. I iterated with the architects, validated changes against source CAD, and kept changes small and reversible. When a glass-material optimization altered the intended appearance, I changed the optimization approach rather than weakening the design requirement.

Outcome

The simulation was delivered and used by the client's architects and lead designer to inform design decisions. The work led to a follow-on mobility simulation initiative. The broader project was later paused due to budget cuts, after the simulation had been delivered and used.

Technical success is not just making a simulation run. It is making it trustworthy enough for the people using it to make real decisions.

What this demonstrates

  • Working closely with demanding domain experts.
  • Balancing runtime performance with fidelity and user intent.
  • Turning a visual prototype into a structured decision-support tool.
  • Validating engineering trade-offs with the people accountable for the outcome.
